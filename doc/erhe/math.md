# erhe_math

Stability: stable

## Purpose
Collection of math utilities for 3D graphics: AABB and bounding sphere
types, viewport projection/unprojection, input axis smoothing, and various
vector/matrix helper functions used throughout erhe.

## Key Types
- `Aabb` -- axis-aligned bounding box with include/transform/query operations
- `Sphere` -- bounding sphere with enclosure, containment, and transform; includes `optimal_enclosing_sphere()`
- `Viewport` -- integer viewport rectangle with project/unproject and hit-test
- `Input_axis` -- smoothed input axis with damping, used for camera movement controls

## Public API
- `Aabb`: `include(point)`, `include(aabb)`, `transformed_by(mat4)`, `center()`, `diagonal()`, `volume()`
- `Sphere`: `enclose(point)`, `enclose(sphere)`, `contains(point)`, `transformed_by(mat4)`, `optimal_enclosing_sphere(points)`. The enclosing sphere is minimal up to float rounding and always contains every input point under the exact `contains(point)` test, at any scale and at any distance from the origin. The support search (MathGeoLib's) keeps every tolerance relative to the coordinate magnitude and the size of the support simplex; the two, three, four and five point cases return the smallest of their grown candidates (circumscribed sphere when its center lies inside the simplex, else the minimal spheres of the sub-simplices grown to reach the left-out point), so a candidate that rounding leaves short costs at most the rounding error; a final pass encloses any point the search's cycle guard left outside. `test/test_sphere.cpp` covers trivial, degenerate and hard cases at several scales and offsets, a Bistro mesh 6 km from the origin, and a seeded pseudorandom smoke test (`ERHE_SPHERE_SMOKE_SECONDS`, `ERHE_SPHERE_SMOKE_SEED`) that checks exact enclosure and Jung's bound on the radius and prints failing inputs as hexadecimal floats.
- `Viewport`: `project_to_screen_space()`, `unproject()`, `aspect_ratio()`, `hit_test()`
- `euler_angles.hpp`: Euler angles to and from quaternions for all 12 axis orders, without a rotation matrix in between. `euler_angles_to_quaternion()` / `quaternion_to_euler_angles()` take the three axis indices; `quatEulerAngleABC()` / `extractEulerAngleABC()` are the per-order forms, named after the matrix functions `glm::eulerAngleABC()` / `glm::extractEulerAngleABC()` whose convention they share (`R_A(t1) * R_B(t2) * R_C(t3)`). Extraction keeps the quaternion's sign: composing the extracted angles gives q back, not -q. `quaternion_to_euler_angles_near()` takes reference angles and returns, of every angle triple that gives q, the one nearest to them, each angle in (-2 pi, 2 pi]; an editor passes the angles it shows, so they survive a round trip through the quaternion as edited. The header comment states the angle ranges and the gimbal-lock rule; `test/test_euler_angles.cpp` covers them.
- `coordinate_conventions.hpp`: `Coordinate_conventions` and its enums (`Depth_range`, `Framebuffer_origin`, `Texture_origin`, `Clip_space_y_flip`), the per-backend conventions a `Device_info` carries; a header with no glm or profile dependency, included by `math_util.hpp`.
- `box_hull.hpp`: `calculate_bounding_convex_hull_of_boxes()` returns the 2D convex hull of the projected corners of many `Aabb`s plus extra points, bit-identical to hulling every projected corner but without sorting the corners of boxes that lie inside the hull of the others (a polygon from the extreme corners in 16 directions, then a center and extents test per box). `Box_hull_scratch` holds the persistent buffers. The shadow fit's rotation stage uses it; `test/test_box_hull.cpp` checks exact equality with the plain hull.
- `curve_path.hpp`: the curve path (next section) and the general-degree Bezier and parallel-transport helpers the sweep shape (`doc/erhe/geometry.md`) evaluates through: `bezier_point()`, `bezier_unit_tangent()` (a finite difference where the derivative vanishes), `initial_transport_up()` (+Y taken off the tangent, +X when the tangent is vertical) and `transport_up()` (the previous up turned by the minimal rotation between the tangents, re-orthogonalized). Callers pass a scratch span of at least the control point count.
- `math_util.hpp`: `remap()`, `unproject<T>()`, `project_to_screen_space<T>()`, color conversion (`vec3_from_uint`, `uint_from_vector3`), axis helpers (`min_axis`, `max_axis`), predefined rotation/swap matrices

## Curve path
`curve_path.hpp` holds 3D curves stepped by distance (`doc/plans/curves.md` C2, C3).

- `Curve_point`: a position, an in and an out handle stored as offsets from the position (moving a point carries its handles), and a `Curve_point_kind`: `corner` (its handles are ignored), `smooth` (the handles stay on one line) or `free`. `Curve_path` is an ordered list of points and a `Curve_closure` (`open` or `closed`); a closed path has one span per point, the last returning to the first.
- Editing helpers: `set_curve_handle()` sets one handle and, on a smooth point, turns the other opposite keeping its length; `get_catmull_rom_handles()` gives handles of a third of the Catmull-Rom tangent (half the neighbor difference, one-sided at the ends of an open path); `set_curve_point_kind()` zeroes the handles when a point becomes a corner, gives Catmull-Rom handles when a corner becomes smooth or free, and turns the in handle opposite the out handle (keeping its length) when a free point becomes smooth.
- Spans (`Curve_span`, `make_curve_span()`): a cubic Bezier p0, p0 + out, p1 + in, p1 from the handles each end contributes (`get_effective_handle()`: none for a corner or a zero handle). A span neither end contributes a handle to is a `line`, evaluated as the exact segment at uniform speed; a span with a handle at one end only puts the other end's inner control point a third of the way towards the handle point, so the span leaves that end pointing at it. `get_span_position()`, `get_span_derivative()` and `get_span_tangent()` evaluate by parameter; at a cusp the tangent is the chord across a 1e-3 parameter step.
- `Curve_path_table` is the baked form: `build()` copies the spans and the arc-length table (containers cleared, capacity kept); it is rebuilt when the points change and no query allocates. Each span is divided into 16 equal parameter intervals whose lengths are integrated in double precision with 5-point Gauss-Legendre, halving an interval (to depth 12) until its halves agree with the whole to 1e-10 relative, so a kink in the speed near a cusp stays accurate; the cumulative lengths are stored as float, which bounds the length error to float rounding (the tests hold 1e-6 relative on a parabola against its analytic length, a quarter circle approximation and a cusp span against double precision references). `locate()` maps a distance to a `Curve_location` (span, t): a binary search over the cumulative lengths, then for a cubic span Newton steps on the in-interval integral until it is within 1e-6 of the interval length; a line span maps linearly.
- Distances clamp to [0, length] on an open path and wrap into [0, length) on a closed one (`normalize_distance()`). A distance exactly at a control point takes the span that leaves the point, so its tangent is the leaving one; the end of an open path takes the arriving span. `get_point_distances()` gives the distance of each span's start point followed by the length, so a consumer places an element at a corner by those distances. `get_distance()` maps a location back to its distance.
- Frames (`Curve_frame`: position, tangent, side, up, with side = cross(up, tangent), the sweep's convention). The up-keeping frame (`make_up_frame()`, `frame_at_distance()`) takes up as the reference up with the tangent's part removed; where the tangent is within 1e-6 of parallel to the reference up, it keeps the given side (the previous heading) made perpendicular to the tangent. `get_up_frames_at_distances()` fills one frame per distance, each vertical sample keeping the previous sample's side and leading vertical samples taking the side of the first non-vertical one. `get_transported_frames_at_distances()` fills parallel-transported frames (ascending distances) with the sweep's `initial_transport_up()` / `transport_up()`.
- Point edits: `insert_curve_point()` splits a span at a location with de Casteljau's construction, so the shape is unchanged: a line span gets a corner (two line spans), a cubic span a smooth point whose handles are the split's inner points; an end that contributed no handle to the span (a corner or a zero handle) gets the split's handle and becomes free, a corner's other handle zeroed so its other span stays straight. `make_curve_end_point()` gives the point that extends an open path at a `Curve_end`: along the end tangent by the end span's chord, a corner beyond a corner end, else smooth with handles a third of that chord; `add_curve_point()` puts a point before the first or after the last. `Curve_path_table::get_nearest_location()` finds the location nearest to a point (64 samples per span, then a golden section search between the neighboring samples).
- `test/test_curve_path.cpp` covers the lengths, the distance mapping, corners, wrapping, the helpers, the frames and the point edits (an insert keeps the shape within 1e-5 on line, cubic, corner-ended and closing spans).

## Dependencies
- `erhe::profile` -- profiling macros (in math_util.hpp)
- External: glm

## Notes
- Most functions in `math_util.hpp` are templated on `float`/`double` via a `vector_types<T>` trait.
- `Input_axis` supports both linear and multiplicative damping modes.
- The unproject/project functions follow OpenGL conventions (configurable depth range).
